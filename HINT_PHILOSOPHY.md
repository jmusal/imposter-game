# Hint Philosophy

## The Problem
Many hints were still too obvious because they describe the word directly:
- Baseball → "home run" (a baseball term - too direct!)
- China → "great wall" (describes China specifically)
- Roosevelt → "new deal" (literally FDR's policy)

## The Solution
Hints should be:
1. **Abstract** - Not a direct clue someone would give
2. **Context clues** - Technical terms, feelings, or tangential details
3. **Deducible** - Players can figure it out if they know the word

## Examples of GOOD hints:
- Baseball → "diamond" (the field shape, but not the word)
- Tennis → "deuce" (tennis terminology, but doesn't say tennis)
- Surfing → "barrel" (specific moment, not "wave")
- China → "dynasties" (era-specific, not "great wall")
- Football → "yards" (measurement, not "touchdown")

## Examples of BAD hints (too obvious):
- Pizza → "cheese" (literally the main ingredient!)
- Television → "remote" (the defining object)
- Dog → "bark" (what dogs do - the most obvious clue)
- Home Run (that IS a baseball clue!)

## Implementation
When reviewing word banks:
- Replace "main ingredient/feature" with "tangential detail"
- Replace "direct action" with "result/feeling"
- Replace "named concept" with "context/terminology"
- Use technical jargon when possible (deuce, mogul, nock, etc.)

This makes it harder but fair - imposters must actually know the subject area!
